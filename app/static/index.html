How it works

From stethoscope to doctor in four steps.

  1. 01

    Record

    Pick a held-out dataset sample, upload a file, or record with the phone microphone. Large files shrink to 4 kHz on the phone.

  2. 02

    Check

    Too short, clipped or silent audio is rejected. An IsolationForest turns away sound that is not from a stethoscope.

  3. 03

    Analyse

    Five LightGBM heads score heart and lung windows. Rules join them: murmur with crackles suggests checking for heart failure.

  4. 04

    Hand off

    Four intake questions, an emergency check that points to 999 or 111, then a summary lands in the doctor's passcode inbox.

    Results

    Honest numbers, held out by patient.

    0.882Heart normal / abnormalMAcc · CinC 2016 adults · top published ~0.86
    0.724Heart murmurMAcc · CirCor children
    0.508Lung sounds, 4 classesICBHI score · state of the art ~0.60 to 0.65
    0.433Lung diagnosis, 6 groupsMacro-F1 · ICBHI + KAUH

    Published figures use other test sets, so they are context, not a contest. The lung sound model is the weak spot. An ImageNet CNN (0.510), an ensemble (0.511) and AudioSet AST transformers (0.533 full, 0.519 slimmed to 70 ms) all scored higher, but a patient-level bootstrap puts every gain within noise for 49 test patients, so the live app keeps the fast model.     Roadmap

    Where it goes next.

    1. Now

      Prototype

      Five models, a phone web app, scripted intake and a doctor inbox.

    2. Next

      Better models

      Fine-tune the AudioSet transformer on a GPU for lung sounds, and add a detector that rejects noise.

    3. Then

      IoT stethoscope

      A device posts to the same API, and paired heart and lung data allow a joint model.

    4. Pilot

      NHS pilot

      HRA ethics approval, local validation and real clinician accounts.

    5. Later

      Regulated product

      DTAC, DCB0129 and DCB0160, then MHRA software as a medical device.
